    screen¶

    phu screen finds the contigs that carry the protein families you ask for. It predicts the proteins of your contigs, searches them with profile HMMs, and keeps the contigs whose proteins match.

    Use it to pull viral contigs out of a metagenomic assembly by their marker proteins, to find contigs that carry a gene of interest, or to collect the proteins behind a family for a new model.

    phu screen -i contigs.fa PF00589                  # one Pfam family
phu screen -i contigs.fa phrog_216 ecod:2004.1    # a PHROG and an ECOD homology group
phu screen -i contigs.fa my-models/*.hmm          # your own HMMs

    The matching contigs are written to phu-screen/matched-contigs.fna.

    Input

    phu screen takes DNA contigs (FASTA). If you already have proteins, search them with pyHMMER or HMMER directly.

    What you can search for¶

    Each query is one of your own HMM files or a family named from the phu databases. You can mix kinds in one run.

    Query Example What it names When a hit counts
    Your HMM file capsid.hmm The model(s) in the file Aligns to more than 35% of the model (--min-hmm-coverage)
    Pfam PF00589, PF00589.17 A Pfam family Pfam's curated gathering cutoff (--cut-ga)
    KOfam K00001 A KEGG Orthology group The KO's own score threshold (--use-kofam-thresholds)
    PHROG phrog_216 A PHROG family (phrog_1 to phrog_38880) 60 bits, and half of both model and protein aligned (--use-phrog-thresholds)
    ECOD ecod:2004.1.1.5, ecod:2004.1 An ECOD family, or every family in a larger group Half of the model aligned (--use-ecod-thresholds)
    dbCAN family GH128, cbm89 A CAZyme family (case does not matter) Domain E-value below 1e-15 and more than 35% of the model aligned
    dbCAN PUL PUL0621 The CAZyme families of one PUL Every family found on the same contig

    Every hit must also have an E-value of at most 1e-6 (--max-evalue). --min-protein-coverage 0.5, off by default, further requires every hit to cover half of its protein, whatever the model; this drops single domains found in long multi-domain proteins. The first query that names a database family installs the database bundle, if it is not installed yet (see Databases). The full rules are in Screen thresholds.

    Pfam and KOfam¶

    Pfam accessions may carry a version (PF00589.17 is read as PF00589). Pfam models keep their curated gathering (GA) cutoffs, also when queried together with other kinds of model; --no-cut-ga turns them off and leaves only --max-evalue and --min-bitscore. KOfam models use the per-KO threshold and score type (full sequence or domain) from KOfam's ko_list; --no-use-kofam-thresholds turns them off.

    PHROG¶

    PHROGs are phage protein families. PHROG has no curated per-model cutoffs, so a hit must score at least 60 bits and align to at least half of both the model and the protein, the rule phu avger uses. --no-use-phrog-thresholds drops that rule. The function and category of each queried PHROG are printed and recorded in .phu/query.json.

    To pick PHROGs by name or category instead of number, give a group: alias:<name>, category:<name> or 'annot:<annotation>', the queries of phu phrogs resolve. Each group is one query, however many PHROGs it selects, so this keeps contigs with a major capsid and a terminase hit:

    phu screen -i contigs.fa --combine-mode all alias:mcp alias:terl

    The PHROGs each group selected are recorded under phrog_groups in .phu/query.json. A PHROG selected by two groups counts for both. Piping phu phrogs resolve --ids-only instead turns every PHROG into its own query.

    ECOD¶

    ECOD classifies protein domains by evolutionary relationship, at four levels. You can query any of them with ecod: and a dotted id:

    Level Example Meaning
    F (family) ecod:2004.1.1.5 One family: one model
    T (topology) ecod:2004.1.1 Every family with this fold topology
    H (homology) ecod:2004.1 Every family thought to share an ancestor
    X (possible homology) ecod:2004 Every family in the broadest group

    The bundle has one model per F-group, 32,041 in ECOD v295.2, built from ECOD's representative domains. Group and family names are in families.tsv of the installed ecod database, and hits name their model as ecod:<family id>.

    ECOD models describe single domains, often within longer proteins, so a hit must align to at least half of the model but may cover any part of the protein. --no-use-ecod-thresholds drops that rule. .phu/query.json records what each ecod: id expanded to, and the names and Pfam accessions of every family searched.

    Broad groups search many models

    ecod:2004.1, the P-loop homology group, expands to 556 families. Expect one domain table per family in the output folder; turn them off with --no-keep-domtbl.

    dbCAN families and PULs¶

    dbCAN family names are matched against the installed dbCAN models, whatever their case. A PUL id expands to the CAZyme families of that PUL (from dbCAN-PUL's cazymes_predicted_dbcan), and by default every family must be found on the same contig; use --combine-mode threshold to accept part of the signature. Only one PUL can be queried per run, and not together with other queries. To search all CAZymes or all PULs at once, see CAZymes and PULs.

    A PUL match means its CAZyme families occur together on a contig. It does not show that the contig carries that PUL or predict its substrate.

    Combining queries¶

    With several queries, --combine-mode decides which contigs are kept:

    Mode Keeps a contig when Use it for
    any (default) At least one query hits Collecting everything related to any query
    all Every query hits Requiring a set of markers together
    threshold At least --min-hmm-hits queries hit Requiring, for example, 3 of 5 markers

    Each ecod: id counts as one query, met by a hit to any of its families: --combine-mode all ecod:2004.1.1 PF00589 keeps contigs with some family of that T-group and PF00589.

    The same holds for your own HMM files: in the default --hmm-mode pure, a file is one query even when it holds several models. To ask for "two of three markers" when each marker has several profiles, put each marker's profiles in one file:

    cat terl/*.hmm > TerL.hmm; cat mcp/*.hmm > MCP.hmm; cat portal/*.hmm > portal.hmm
phu screen -i contigs.fa --combine-mode threshold --min-hmm-hits 2 TerL.hmm MCP.hmm portal.hmm

    A hit to any profile in TerL.hmm counts once, as TerL. With --hmm-mode mixed, every model counts as its own query instead.

    For each kept contig, the best hit of each matched query is reported (--top-per-contig raises this). all and threshold check the queries you gave, not the biology: a contig with every marker you asked for is not proof of a complete genome, so record the exact queries with your results.

    Output files¶

    File or folder Written when Content
    matched-contigs.fna always The contigs that passed
    matched-contig-ids.txt always Their ids, one per line
    {model}-hmmsearch-domains.tbl --keep-domtbl (default) hmmsearch per-domain table of each model
    .phu/query.json always, except --all-cazymes / --all-puls The query, how it was expanded and matched, and for database families the bundle version, file hashes and family names
    .phu/run.json --all-cazymes / --all-puls Run manifest with counts, thresholds and files written
    query-hmms/ database queries The models taken from the databases
    predicted-proteins.faa --keep-proteins Every predicted protein
    .phu/prediction.json --keep-proteins Where the cached proteins are and their cache key
    target-proteins/{model}.faa --save-target-proteins Matched proteins of each model, from kept contigs only
    target-hmms/{model}.hmm --save-target-hmms An HMM built from each model's matched proteins

    Proteins are named <contig>|gene<n>; contig names may themselves contain |.

    How models are named in output files

    {model} is the model id made safe for file names (unusual characters become _). For your own HMMs in the default --hmm-mode pure, each file is one query and its file name (without extension) is the model id, also when the file holds several models. If two files share a name (a/foo.hmm, b/foo.hmm), the NAME inside each HMM tells them apart; if that is identical too, phu warns and pools their hits. If two different models would get the same file name (beta/1 and beta_1), phu refuses the query rather than mix their results.

    With --hmm-mode mixed, each model in a file is its own query (for example a concatenated database), and outputs are written per model name inside the file.

    Saving matched proteins and building HMMs¶

    --save-target-proteins writes the proteins that matched each model, taken only from contigs that were kept, one file per model.

    --save-target-hmms (with --save-target-proteins) builds a new HMM from each model's matched proteins: they are aligned with FAMSA and the HMM is built with pyHMMER, all inside phu. A model with a single protein gets a single-sequence HMM. Only the proteins of the current run are used.

    --trim-target-msa trims each alignment with trimAl before the HMM is built:

    Value What is removed
    none Nothing (default)
    gappyout Columns judged too gappy from the alignment's own gap distribution; the mildest choice
    strict Gappy columns and columns with low residue similarity
    strictplus As strict, with a tighter similarity criterion; the most aggressive

    Trimming shortens the model and lowers bit scores, so cutoffs chosen for untrimmed models do not carry over. The run prints the columns before and after (gappyout: 1523 -> 377 columns). If trimming leaves no columns or fewer than two sequences, the untrimmed alignment is used, with a warning.

    These HMMs are built from whatever matched, so check them before reusing them as references.

    CAZymes and PULs¶

    Two options search the whole dbCAN collection instead of a query.

    --all-cazymes searches every CAZyme family (AA, CBM, CE, GH, GT and PL, keeping subfamilies such as GH5_2; helper modules such as SLH, cohesin and dockerin are left out) and keeps contigs with at least one family hit.

    phu-screen/
├── matched-contigs.fna
├── matched-contig-ids.txt
├── cazyme-matches.tsv.gz        # one row per contig and family
├── cazyme-family-summary.tsv    # contigs and proteins per family
├── cazyme-class-summary.tsv     # contigs and proteins per class
├── evidence/
│   └── cazyme-hits.tsv.gz       # every hit that passed
└── .phu/
    └── run.json

    --all-puls checks every dbCAN-PUL signature: a contig is kept when all families of at least one PUL occur on it. Each family is searched once. PULs that name families missing from the installed dbCAN models are skipped and listed in .phu/run.json; that reflects the database, not your contigs.

    phu-screen/
├── matched-contigs.fna
├── matched-contig-ids.txt
├── pul-matches.tsv.gz           # one row per contig and matched PUL
├── pul-family-support.tsv.gz    # the family hits behind each match
├── pul-summary.tsv
├── pul-substrate-summary.tsv
├── evidence/
│   └── cazyme-hits.tsv.gz
└── .phu/
    └── run.json

    The CAZyme evidence is kept even when no PUL matches; the PUL tables then have only their headers. The per-family HMMs and domain tables of these runs are temporary and removed at the end.

    Counts in the summaries are occurrences, not normalized abundances. The substrates in pul-substrate-summary.tsv are those annotated for the reference PULs, not predictions for your contigs. This is targeted HMM screening of CAZyme families, not a full run_dbcan annotation.

    How it works¶

    1. Predict proteins with pyrodigal-gv, which handles the compact genes and alternative genetic codes of phages (--mode meta, the default, for mixed contigs; single for one genome). Proteins shorter than --min-protein-len-aa are dropped. Predictions are cached and reused while the contigs and prediction settings are unchanged (see Cache handling).
    2. Search the proteins with each model using pyHMMER.
    3. Filter the hits with the cutoffs of each model's kind, then keep contigs by --combine-mode.
    4. Write the kept contigs and, if asked, their proteins and new HMMs.

    Everything runs inside phu: no HMMER, seqkit or aligner program is needed.

    Examples¶

    Keep contigs that carry both a major head protein and a terminase large subunit (PHROG):

    phu screen -i contigs.fa --combine-mode all phrog_10 phrog_2

    Keep contigs with at least 3 of 4 marker HMMs, and build new HMMs from what matched:

    phu screen -i metagenome.fa --combine-mode threshold --min-hmm-hits 3 \
    --save-target-proteins --save-target-hmms \
    marker1.hmm marker2.hmm marker3.hmm marker4.hmm

    Search a whole ECOD homology group, without the per-family domain tables:

    phu screen -i contigs.fa --no-keep-domtbl ecod:2004.1

    Be stricter, and use 16 threads:

    phu screen -i contigs.fa --max-evalue 1e-10 --threads 16 protein_family.hmm

    Turn off Pfam's curated cutoffs and use your own score:

    phu screen -i contigs.fa --no-cut-ga --min-bitscore 30 PF00001

    Quote paths that contain shell characters:

    phu screen -i 'scaffolds|assembly.fa' protein_family.hmm

    Options¶

     Usage: phu screen [OPTIONS] [QUERY]...                                         

 Find contigs that carry given protein families.                                

 Proteins are predicted from the contigs and searched with each QUERY: your     
 own HMM files, or families from the phu databases by name: Pfam (PF00001),     
 KOfam (K00001), PHROG (phrog_216, or a group: alias:mcp, category:tail,        
 'annot:<annotation>'; find them with `phu phrogs`), ECOD                       
 (ecod:2004.1.1.5 for one family, or a T, H or X group such as ecod:2004.1.1    
 for all its families), dbCAN (GH128) or one dbCAN PUL (PUL0621). Each          
 database's own cutoffs apply. A PHROG group or ECOD group counts as one        
 query in --combine-mode all and threshold.                                     

 Examples:                                                                      
     phu screen -i contigs.fa PF00001 PF00589                                   
     phu screen -i contigs.fa my-models/*.hmm                                   
     phu screen -i contigs.fa --combine-mode all K00001 K00002                  
     phu screen -i contigs.fa --combine-mode all alias:mcp alias:terl           
     phu screen -i contigs.fa --all-cazymes                                     

 Full guide: https://camilogarciabotero.github.io/phu/commands/screen/
